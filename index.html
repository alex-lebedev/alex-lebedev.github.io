Why clinical AI fails

Clinical AI fails when model performance is mistaken for clinical performance.

Most damage shows up after launch: workflows and data shift, quality erodes, and the system becomes hard to trace, debug, and own operationally.

  • The clinical job moves—protocols, coding, eligibility, and product paths diverge from what you validated; the “target” is a moving one on both sides.
  • Silent degradation in production—inputs and populations shift; without monitoring tied to harm, performance drops before anyone notices.
  • No audit trail, no incident response—you cannot reconstruct data lineage, model version, prompts/tools, and config—so failures are not explainable or fixable.
  • Integration is where architecture dies—latency, missing fields, upstream changes, and human-in-the-loop behavior break assumptions baked into training and evaluation.
  • Paper governance vs operating governance—policies exist, but there are no runbooks, change control, drift triggers, or clear accountability when something breaks.
  • Evaluation optimizes the wrong thing—leaderboard metrics or narrow test sets that do not match the decision, edge cases, or failure modes the product actually sees.

If you cannot explain what the system is allowed to do, how you know it is safe enough, and what happens when it is wrong, you do not have a clinical system. You have a demo.

How I work

I start from the clinical job and the harm surface. Then I design controls the team can actually run.

Typical sequence:

  • Use case and claims: who uses it, for what decision, under what constraints
  • Risk map: harm taxonomy, misuse, edge cases, “never events”
  • Architecture: data lineage, model boundaries, tooling, audit trails, reliability constraints
  • Evaluation: reference standards, test sets, failure‑mode inventory, go/no‑go criteria
  • Operations: monitoring signals, drift response, incident handling, change management

Typical requests

Examples of what teams bring—scoped, written, and tied to a concrete decision.

Is our evaluation defensible for launch?

Claims, datasets, failure modes, and the gaps you have not tested—before you commit in front of clinicians, customers, or regulators.

Will this architecture survive real customers, integrations, and production operations?

Data paths, versioning, reliability, monitoring hooks, and clear ownership when pipelines and products change—beyond a pilot or demo environment.

We are shipping an LLM in a health product—what breaks first?

Boundaries, tool use, escalation, red-team coverage, and continuous checks—not prompt theater.

We are investing—what is real?

Evidence versus demo, deployment realism, and the shortest list of risks that can kill the thesis—for boards, funds, and executive teams.
